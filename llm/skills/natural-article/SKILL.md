# Natural article style

Write connected explanations that help readers understand why an idea matters, how it works, and what follows from it.

- Establish the reader, purpose, and assumed knowledge from the request and source material. For revisions, read the passages and surrounding context before editing.
- Open with a concrete problem, observation, or useful question. Introduce terminology through the explanation instead of leading with a glossary.
- Connect cause, mechanism, and consequence. Let each paragraph develop one idea and give the next paragraph a reason to follow.
- Use precise verbs, familiar words, and varied sentence lengths. Explain with concrete examples; label hypothetical situations. Keep qualifications close to the claims they limit.
- Use tables for comparisons and lists for steps or parallel items. Turn disconnected declarations into prose. Vary openings rather than adding an anecdote to every section.
- Apply the style throughout the requested text. Remove repetition and filler; readability should come from clearer reasoning, not extra length.
- Preserve facts, caveats, versions, examples, and citations. Verify new factual claims. For revisions, preserve unrelated edits and keep code, tables, headings, and link targets intact unless restructuring is requested.

Before delivery, read for flow, compare against the source for lost qualifications, and check Markdown structure and local links. Report the scope and checks actually performed.
